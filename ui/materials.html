Materials evidence · reviewed November 23, 2026

Retainer and aligner materials

Prefer a traceable dental product with evidence for its intended use and finished process, selected with your dental professional. The evidence reviewed here does not establish one best polymer.

Match the material to the purpose

PETG / copolyester sheets
Used in dental appliances. Check the exact product's indication, formed thickness, wear and dimensional stability.
Polyurethane and multilayer sheets
Product formulations differ. Active aligners need force-retention evidence; passive retainers need shape stability over their intended wear period.
PHA biopolymers
A manufacturer markets a PHA retainer and reports no added plasticizers, colorants or stabilizers. This is not evidence that generic PHA is suitable for retainers or active aligners.

See a manufacturer's indication guide, a thermoforming and aging study, and the PHA retainer description.

What the microplastic evidence tells us

A manufacturer-authored laboratory whitepaper measured particles released by PETG under simulated wear. It did not test PHA or human health outcomes. It does not establish a safest material or a reason to stop prescribed retainer wear.

Biodegradable, BPA-free and “not detected” claims do not establish overall safety. Ask which batch, substances, detection limits and exposure conditions were tested.

Ask before choosing a product

  • Is it intended for a passive retainer or an active aligner?
  • What are the manufacturer, product, composition, lot and thickness?
  • Does the evidence cover the finished appliance after forming or printing, finishing and cleaning?
  • What are its wear, replacement, cleaning and storage instructions?
  • What evidence is available for biocompatibility, wear particles and performance over time?

Use the exact product's instructions. Model-printing resin and oral-contact appliance material are separate choices. A printable shell file does not validate a resin or manufacturing process.

Finished-device biocompatibility evidence matters. FDA registration alone is not authorization.
