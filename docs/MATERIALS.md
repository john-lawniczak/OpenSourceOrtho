# Retainer and aligner materials

Reviewed 2026-11-23. OpenSource Ortho is a clear-aligner planning safety
playground and research toolkit. Material names in a handoff are descriptive;
they do not establish suitability for physical use.

## What to prefer

Prefer a traceable dental product with evidence for the intended appliance,
contact duration, and finished manufacturing process, selected with the treating
dental professional. There is no universally preferred polymer established by
the sources reviewed here. Passive retention and active tooth movement require
different performance evidence.

| Material family | Evidence to request |
| --- | --- |
| PETG / copolyester sheets | Exact product indication, formed thickness, dimensional stability, wear and aging results. PETG is used in dental sheets, but products and processes are not interchangeable. |
| Polyurethane and multilayer sheets | Exact composition and indication; force retention and stress relaxation for active aligners, and long-term shape stability for retainers. |
| PHA (polyhydroxyalkanoates) | Exact formulation, finished-appliance biocompatibility, degradation conditions and products, wear, and clinical performance. A biodegradable polymer family is not a blanket endorsement. |

Primary examples: [PETG/PU thermoforming and aging experiment](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11330653/),
[manufacturer material-selection guide](https://www.zenduradental.com/resources/material_selection_guide/),
and [product-specific instructions](https://www.zenduradental.com/resources/ifu/).
These are evidence references, not a shopping ranking. Model-printing resin and
the appliance's oral-contact material are separate selections; a model resin
designation does not establish suitability for a directly printed tray.

## Moses review

[Moses describes](https://moses.com/products/unplastic-retainer) its retainer as
pure PHA without added plasticizers, colorants, or stabilizers. Its
[process description](https://moses.com/pages/learn-more) describes microbial
fermentation and thermoforming PHA discs over models. The exact polymer grade
is not identified in the reviewed pages. These are manufacturer statements.
Its [FAQ](https://moses.com/pages/faq) distinguishes retainers from active aligners.

The [September 22, 2026 whitepaper](https://moses.com/cdn/shop/files/microplastic-whitepaper.pdf),
by Moses science lead Andrea H. Westlie, tests PETG, not PHA. Four posterior
upper/lower specimen pairs underwent 58,240 loading cycles in water. Mean mass
loss was 3.35 ± 0.90 mg; three LDIR samples yielded 739 ± 449 particles/mL
(15–500 µm range), or approximately 59,100 particles per pair. One antagonist
disconnected. The annual estimate is extrapolated. This small laboratory study
does not measure human absorption, health outcomes, PHA superiority, or all
retainer products. Its limitations include simulated loading and omitted saliva
chemistry, cleaning, and patient variation. It cannot establish retainers as
the leading source of bodily microplastics.

The site's batch screening and simulated-gut degradation claims are separate
from this whitepaper. Preserve the tested batch, detection limits, and test
conditions when recording such claims; a non-detect does not mean zero.
Neither biodegradability nor food-use language establishes oral-appliance safety.
[FDA biocompatibility guidance](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/biocompatibility-assessment-resource-center/glossary-biocompatibility-terms)
distinguishes food-use status from device-specific assessment. Also,
[FDA registration is not authorization](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/dental-devices/clear-dental-aligners).

## Application changes and follow-up

The app now links to an offline-readable materials guide from retainer reference
preparation and both print handoff entry points. It provides evidence questions,
separates material families from product selection, and labels the PHA evidence
gap. It does not auto-select a material, change movement settings, or grant
export readiness based on material choice.

For a future structured materials record, capture manufacturer/product, lot,
composition, intended appliance, nominal and formed thickness, instructions
revision, processing and cleaning requirements, replacement guidance, test
report links, and reviewer/date. Keep manufacturer claims, laboratory results,
and clinical evidence distinct; unknown evidence must stay unknown. Material
performance should not change simulated tooth motion without a separately
validated model.
